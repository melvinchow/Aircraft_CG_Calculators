Delta Canard CG Calculator

Three tiers of increasing approximation: a reliable linear neutral point, an approximate vortex-lift AoA migration, and an illustrative wing-loading trim coupling. Read each tier's caveats. Use consistent units (mm for lengths, mm² implied for areas; the %MAC outputs are unit-independent).

Tier 1 — what the inputs & outputs mean on the airframe

Diagram

Top-down view of the left half of a Rafale-style delta canard. Forward is up, aft is down; the fuselage centerline is on the right. Blue = measured inputs, orange = computed geometry, green = aerodynamic centers, red = neutral point and CG.

Blue — measured inputs (chords, spans, sweep)
Orange — computed geometry (L_c, sweep, static margin)
Green — aerodynamic centers (25% MAC of each surface)
Red — neutral point & CG
Reading the diagram: the wing carries far more lift than the canard (bigger area, and lift-curve slope × area dominates), so the lift-weighted neutral point X_np sits much closer to the wing AC than to the canard AC — not halfway between them. Your CG goes just forward of X_np, by the static margin you choose. A bigger canard or a longer L_c drags X_np forward, forcing the CG forward too.

Tier 2 — Aerodynamic-center migration with AoA

Approximate

Uses the Polhamus leading-edge-suction analogy to estimate how the wing's aerodynamic center drifts aft as angle of attack increases and vortex lift develops. The shift is not a constant per degree — it accelerates because vortex lift scales with sin²(α). Trustworthy ~0–15°, shaky 15–25° as vortex breakdown nears, unreliable beyond. Output shown both as absolute %MAC and as shift from the 0° baseline.

Inputs
Where lift acts in attached flow. ~25% for typical airfoils; ~30–35% for slender deltas.
Where the vortex lift acts — further aft, typically 45–55%.
Where the leading-edge vortex bursts and lift collapses. Higher for sharper, more swept wings. ~25–35° typical; lower for low sweep.
Departure warning: past the breakdown AoA the vortex bursts asymmetrically and the aircraft can depart (tumble). Light, low-inertia models tumble hardest. The calculator marks this boundary but cannot predict recovery behavior.

Tier 3 — Wing-loading trim coupling

Illustrative

Connects wing loading → required lift coefficient → trim AoA → AC shift (from Tier 2) → felt static margin. This stacks approximation on approximation, so treat it as a relative comparison tool — "how does my heavier build trim differently from the manufacturer's spec" — not an absolute CG predictor.

Inputs
~1.225 at sea level; ~1.10 at 1500 m / hot day.
Cruise or approach speed you care about. 20 m/s ≈ 72 km/h ≈ 45 mph.
Configuration A (e.g. manufacturer spec)
All-up weight ÷ wing area. Common RC unit.
Configuration B (e.g. your build)
Canard-down / elevon-up trim trick (context, not modeled): setting the canard slightly leading-edge-up and compensating with up-elevon biases lift toward the canard, energizing the wing's upper-surface vortex flow. This raises total C_L at a given AoA, lowering stall speed and tightening turns, at the cost of trim drag. It's a tuning knob on top of CG, not a substitute for it. Modeling it numerically needs control-derivative data this tool doesn't have.

Variable glossary

S_w, S_c
Planform area of wing and canard.
AR
Aspect ratio = span² / area. Higher = more slender; lower = stubbier.
a_w, a_c
Lift-curve slope (lift per radian of AoA) from the Helmbold/DATCOM equation. Per-radian here; divide by 57.3 for per-degree.
η_c
Canard efficiency — dynamic-pressure ratio at the canard. ~1.0 in clean air.
L_root
Measured input. Distance the canard's root leading edge sits ahead of the wing's root leading edge, along the fuselage centerline at the aircraft center. Positive number.
L_c
Derived output. Distance the canard's aerodynamic center sits ahead of the wing's AC, along the centerline. Computed from L_root plus each surface's own sweep-induced MAC offset (X_mac + 0.25·MAC).
X_np
Neutral point — the whole-aircraft aerodynamic center. CG must sit ahead of it for static stability.
Static margin
(X_np − X_cg) / MAC, as a percentage. Positive = stable. The gap you keep between CG and NP.
C_L,p / C_L,v
Potential (attached) and vortex contributions to lift coefficient in the Polhamus model.
Vortex breakdown
AoA at which the leading-edge vortex bursts; lift collapses and the aircraft can depart.
C_L
Lift coefficient — dimensionless lift. C_L = Lift / (½·ρ·V²·S).
Coordinate convention: all %MAC and length outputs are measured aft of the wing's leading edge (at the wing root), along the fuselage centerline, expressed as a fraction of the wing's MAC — consistent with the conventional and complex-wing calculators.
