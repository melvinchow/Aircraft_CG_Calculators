Finding MAC, X_mac, Y_mac, and CG on a Swept Wing

Top-down view of a left wing panel with backward (aft) sweep. Forward is up, aft is down. Fuselage runs vertically on the right. Steps numbered in execution order.

Steps

1Measure Cr at the fuselage centerline. For swept wings, extrapolate the LE and TE lines inward from the fuselage side to the centerline — the chord between those extrapolated points is your Cr.
2Measure Ct — tip chord, parallel to centerline
3Measure b/2 — semi-span, perpendicular from centerline to tip (not along the swept LE)
4Measure Λ — leading-edge sweep angle. 0° if LE is perpendicular to fuselage; positive for backward sweep, negative for forward sweep
5Compute MAC length
MAC = (2/3)·Cr·(1+λ+λ²)/(1+λ)
λ = Ct / Cr
6Compute Y_mac — spanwise distance from centerline to where the MAC "lives"
Y_mac = (b/6)·(1+2λ)/(1+λ)
7Compute X_mac — how far aft of the root LE the MAC's leading edge sits, due to sweep
X_mac = Y_mac · tan(Λ)
Zero for unswept wings. Positive (aft) for backward sweep. Negative (forward) for forward sweep.
8Draw the MAC line at the MAC station, parallel to centerline, from wing LE to wing TE. Its length should equal MAC. The MAC's LE will sit X_mac aft of the root LE. (In the diagram the line is drawn slightly offset to the left for visibility — on your actual wing it lives right on the MAC station, between LE and TE.)
9Mark %MAC point on the MAC line, aft from its LE. Starting values: 25% (safe), 30% (sport), 33%+ (aerobatic).
10Project to centerline — perpendicular from the %MAC point to the fuselage. That intersection is the CG.
CG distance aft of root LE (along centerline) = X_mac + (%MAC × MAC)

Reminder: The "From root LE" column already accounts for sweep. To find the balance point on your model, measure that distance aft from where the root LE meets the fuselage, marked along the fuselage centerline. Always start at a more forward CG for the maiden flight and progressively move aft. Use dive/stall/inverted-trim flight tests to confirm.
