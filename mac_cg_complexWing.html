MAC & CG for Complex Wings — Piecewise Trapezoidal Method

For wings with curved leading or trailing edges (e.g. Sea Fury, Spitfire). Split the semi-span into 2–10 sections; each section is treated as its own trapezoid, then combined via area-weighted averages.

Steps

1Pick n — the number of sections (2 to 10). More sections = better approximation of curved edges. 3 is usually enough for a Sea Fury-style wing; 5 for a Spitfire ellipse.
2Measure b/2 — semi-span, from fuselage centerline to wingtip (or to the equivalent linearized tip if you've eyeballed the rounded tip into a straight one).
3Measure chord at each station — c₀ at centerline (extrapolated through fuselage), c₁, c₂, …, cₙ at each section boundary. cₙ is the tip chord (Ct). All chords measured parallel to the fuselage centerline.
4Measure each section's width — Δy₁, Δy₂, …, Δyₙ. The widths must sum to b/2.
★(Optional) Measure sweep angle Λ — single value applied to whole wing. Use 0° for unswept wings with curved tips like the Sea Fury.
5Compute per-section values — for each strip i:
Sᵢ = (cᵢ₋₁ + cᵢ) · Δyᵢ / 2
MACᵢ = (2/3)·(cᵢ₋₁² + cᵢ₋₁·cᵢ + cᵢ²) / (cᵢ₋₁ + cᵢ)
Y_macᵢ = yᵢ₋₁ + Δyᵢ · (cᵢ₋₁ + 2cᵢ) / [3·(cᵢ₋₁ + cᵢ)]
where yᵢ₋₁ is the cumulative distance from centerline to the inboard edge of strip i.
6Combine via area-weighted averages:
MAC = Σ(MACᵢ · Sᵢ) / Σ Sᵢ
Y_mac = Σ(Y_macᵢ · Sᵢ) / Σ Sᵢ
X_mac = Y_mac · tan(Λ)
7Draw combined MAC line at the combined Y_mac station, parallel to centerline. Length = combined MAC. (In the diagram, drawn offset for clarity.)
8Mark %MAC point on the combined MAC line, aft from its LE.
9Project to centerline. CG distance aft of root LE (along centerline) = X_mac + (%MAC × MAC)

Variable glossary

Inputs (what you measure)

n
Number of sections you split the semi-span into (2–10). More sections give a better approximation but require more measurements.
b/2
Semi-span — distance from fuselage centerline to wingtip (or equivalent linearized tip).
cᵢ
Chord length at station i. Station 0 is at the centerline (c₀ = root chord, Cr). Station n is at the tip (cₙ = tip chord, Ct). Total chord stations = n + 1.
Δyᵢ
Width of section i (distance from station i–1 to station i). The Greek letter Δ (delta) means "interval" or "change in". Total sections = n. Widths must sum to b/2.
Λ
Leading-edge sweep angle (capital Greek lambda). Positive for backward sweep, negative for forward sweep, zero for unswept. Single value applied to the whole wing.

Per-section computed values (one per strip)

λᵢ
Local taper ratio of section i (lowercase Greek lambda). λᵢ = cᵢ / cᵢ₋₁.
Sᵢ
Planform area of section i. Sᵢ = (cᵢ₋₁ + cᵢ) · Δyᵢ / 2.
MACᵢ
Mean Aerodynamic Chord of section i, treating it as a standalone trapezoid.
Y_macᵢ
Spanwise location of section i's MAC, measured from the fuselage centerline (not from the strip's inboard edge).
yᵢ
Cumulative spanwise position of station i, measured from centerline. y₀ = 0, yₙ = b/2.

Combined output values

MAC
Combined Mean Aerodynamic Chord — area-weighted average of MACᵢ across all sections. This is the equivalent chord length the whole wing "behaves like" aerodynamically.
Y_mac
Combined spanwise MAC location — area-weighted average of Y_macᵢ. This is where the combined MAC line gets drawn on the wing.
X_mac
How far aft of the root LE the combined MAC's leading edge sits, due to sweep. X_mac = Y_mac · tan(Λ). Zero for unswept wings.

Math symbols

Σ
Capital Greek sigma — "sum across all i". Σ Sᵢ means "sum of S₁ + S₂ + … + Sₙ".
·
Multiplication (interchangeable with ×).
²
Squared (multiplied by itself once).
tan(Λ)
Tangent of the sweep angle. For small angles, tan(Λ) ≈ Λ in radians. Used to convert spanwise position into aft displacement.

Why area-weighted averages?

The general (calculus) definition of MAC is MAC · S = ∫ c(y)² dy, where the integral is taken across the full span. When the wing is split into trapezoidal strips, that integral becomes a sum: ∫ c(y)² dy = Σ (MACᵢ · Sᵢ). Dividing both sides by the total area gives MAC = Σ(MACᵢ · Sᵢ) / Σ Sᵢ — an area-weighted average. The same logic applies to Y_mac, which weights spanwise position by chord. Bigger sections (more area) contribute more to the average, which is physically correct because they generate more lift.

How accurate is this method?

For a Sea Fury-style wing (mild taper, mostly straight LE, curved TE near tip), splitting into 3–5 sections gets you within 1% of the true MAC. The flight-test CG sweep range you'd explore anyway is much larger than that error, so this method is well within practical tolerance. For wings with highly curved planforms (Spitfire ellipse), use 6–10 sections concentrated where the curvature is sharpest.
